## Constraints

**Water.** Distilled water is bought at a convenience store with unknown provenance and treatment. It's used for the espresso machine only — small volumes, and scale protection justifies it. It is *not* trusted for drinking in filter quantities. Third Wave Water is also a cost constraint. Filter brewing therefore uses bottled spring water, and any recommendation that assumes TWW-for-filter is a non-starter.

**Location: Croatia.** Relevant for water sourcing. Local bottled water mineral data is in `principles.md` §6.

**Temperature.** 96 °C is the machine's maximum. Advice built on 98–99 °C — which appears in some turbo literature — cannot be executed here.

## Filter brewing prehistory

Filter was largely avoided before this journal, on the grounds that it seemed *more* unforgiving than espresso and had too many variables. What had been tried, across various coffees:

- 200–250 g water, doses unrecorded
- Temperatures ranging from 80 °C to boiling
- Full immersion for the whole brew
- Half immersion, half percolation
- Straight percolation, V60-style
- Intermittent percolation with the Switch closed for more extraction

Result was described as **"sometimes beautiful and sometimes undrinkable."**

**Two things now explain this**, and both were identified during the El Perezoso work:

1. **Those are five different brew methods across a 20-degree temperature range.** They aren't variations of one recipe; they're different beverages. The inconsistency was structural, not mysterious.
2. **Tap water was used throughout.** Municipal supplies vary seasonally, which alone would produce erratic results. See `principles.md` §6 for the mechanism.

Filter is not more unforgiving than espresso — it's more *permissive*. It will produce liquid no matter what you change, whereas espresso refuses to run at all. That permissiveness reads as chaos when several variables move at once.

## Palate notes

- Reference point for a satisfying cup: **Ethiopia Sidama Yaye** as a turbo shot, on a recipe similar to the early El Perezoso attempts. Notably better than the Costa Rican on comparable parameters.
- **Unresolved:** whether the roaster's "sour cherry" descriptor on El Perezoso reads as pleasant acidity or as plain sourness on this palate. Worth watching across bags — if bright, acid-forward coffees consistently disappoint, that's a preference signal, not a brewing failure.

## Influences

Turbo shot technique came from **Lance Hedrick**'s YouTube channel. The underlying research lineage is the Cameron/Hendon *Matter* (2020) work on systematically improving espresso, which argued for coarser grinds and lower doses on reproducibility and waste-reduction grounds. Worth noting the paper's claims are narrower than the technique's popular framing — it isn't an argument that coarse-and-fast tastes better on every coffee.
